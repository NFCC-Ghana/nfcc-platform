## How to read this document

NFCC has two parts, and the manuscript should keep them distinct:

1. **The operational platform:** the live, rule-based flood-risk and alerting system for 9 tracked districts, running on Google Cloud Run and operated through a Streamlit dashboard (section 2; operating record in section 8).
2. **The research and forecasting framework:** a reproducible machine-learning study of weekly flood onset in all 216 districts of Ghana (FAO GAUL 2015), with an operational model that runs weekly in *shadow mode*: scored and logged, never used for alerts (sections 3–5).

Section 1 gives the verdict and the evidence behind it; section 9 is the findings register and the remediation plan; section 12 lists, claim by claim, what the manuscript may and may not say.

**Terms used throughout:**

- **District-week:** one district in one Monday-to-Sunday week, the unit of analysis.
- **Flood onset:** a district-week that is flooded when the previous week was not; the prediction target.
- **Base rate:** the share of district-weeks that are onsets.
- **PR-AUC:** area under the precision-recall curve (average precision). A model with no skill scores the base rate, so "PR-AUC ÷ base rate" reads as "times better than chance".
- **POD / FAR:** probability of detection / false-alarm ratio.
- **SEDI:** Symmetric Extremal Dependence Index (Ferro & Stephenson 2011): 0 is no skill, 1 is perfect, and it stays meaningful for rare events.
- **Within-district AUC:** ROC-AUC computed inside each district, then averaged; it measures *when* a district floods, not *which* districts flood.
- **Rule score S:** the live platform's 0–100 rainfall index (section 2.3). It is an index, not a probability.

## 1. Executive summary

**Verdict.** The *research framework* is a sound, reproducible study whose results can be published with the caveats in sections 11 and 12. The *live platform* is an engineering prototype, not a warning service, and the manuscript must not describe it as one. Until 4 November 2026 two of its four observation pathways could not work (satellite radar, river and dam levels), a third produced a constant (GloFAS), and its outcome log recorded floods that did not happen; the software fixes for these were deployed on 4 November and the log re-verified. On 5 November the river and dam pathway still has no data (the DAHITI key is rejected), the 3-day rainfall signal is scored on the wrong scale, the alert channel cannot reach the public, the dashboard's privacy setting is not yet confirmed, and no operator has ever reviewed one of its 82 queued assessments. The live platform has **no record of skill**: after re-verification none of its 1,078 checked predictions has a confirmed flood, but almost every prediction was LOW and the evidence sources rarely resolve a district, so the record shows neither hits nor false alarms. The remaining items need the account owner or partner agencies (section 9.3).

**What the evidence supports.**

- **Forecast skill (research, test years 2018–2025, observed rain of the target week).** The best model, M11 (multi-source XGBoost with local-climate rainfall), reaches PR-AUC 0.081 against a base rate of 0.0084 (9.6× chance) and SEDI 0.49. Tested on the same resampled years, its gain over climatology is +0.034 [90% CI +0.016, +0.052] (better; M11 better in 100% of 1,000 resamples), and over the same model without local-climate features +0.009 [90% CI +0.000, +0.016] (marginally better; the interval touches zero). The live rule M3 scores PR-AUC 0.030, worse than climatology (-0.017 [90% CI -0.029, -0.008]).
- **When versus where.** For the *timing* of floods within a district, the rule is as good as any model (within-district AUC 0.80 against 0.78 for M11; paired difference -0.016 [90% CI -0.034, +0.014], not distinguishable). M11's advantage is mostly in knowing *which* districts flood, and built-up share, its leading feature, partly reflects where floods are reported (sections 5.3, 5.8).
- **Lead time.** With only inputs known when the week starts, the deployable model's gain over climatology is +0.004 [90% CI -0.009, +0.019] (not distinguishable; section 5.12). With real archived ECMWF forecasts (2024–2025, two test years), the antecedent-only model reaches PR-AUC 0.089 against 0.060 for climatology, and the live rule applied to the forecast has almost no skill (SEDI 0.07).
- **Fairness.** At its alert threshold M11 detects 31% of urban onsets and 3% of rural ones, with equal timing skill: one national threshold serves the towns and misses the countryside (section 5.13).
- **Operational value.** At its WATCH level (P ≥ 5%) the shadow model would issue about 217 district alerts a year nationally and detect 19% of onsets, with a false-alarm ratio of 0.92. That is a basis for a watch-level advisory to district officers, not for public warnings.
- **Rainfall inputs.** At Ghana's 22 synoptic stations, satellite rainfall agrees poorly with gauges day by day (r 0.26 CHIRPS, 0.33 IMERG) and catches under a third of ≥ 20 mm gauge days, but agrees well week by week (r 0.75–0.76). This supports the weekly design and argues against daily thresholds on satellite rain (section 5.11).
- **Operational use.** None yet: 82 assessments queued since September, none reviewed by an operator; 0 subscribers; 4 citizen reports, all tests (section 8).

### 2.3 Risk scoring (rule-based)

The live risk score `S` (0–100) is a piecewise-linear function of rainfall `P` in mm (`calculate_score`, `src/alerts/formatter.py`):

| Rainfall P (mm) | Score S |
| --- | --- |
| P ≤ 0 | 0 |
| 0 < P < 10 | 3P |
| 10 ≤ P < 30 | 30 + 2(P − 10) |
| 30 ≤ P < 50 | 70 + 1.5(P − 30) |
| P ≥ 50 | min(100, 95 + 0.2(P − 50)) |

**Urban drainage multiplier** (Accra Central, West and East only). The score is multiplied by a drainage factor:

- **Base factor by drainage condition:** CRITICAL 1.5, POOR 1.2, FAIR 1.0, GOOD 0.8.
- **Rainfall factor:** 1.3 for P > 80 mm, 1.1 for P > 50 mm, otherwise 1.0.
- **Other districts:** 1.0 (no data).

**Risk tiers:**

| Tier | Score range |
| --- | --- |
| LOW | S < 30 |
| MODERATE | 30 ≤ S < 50 |
| HIGH | 50 ≤ S < 70 |
| CRITICAL | 70 ≤ S < 85 |
| EXTREME | S ≥ 85 |

(`src/config/settings.py`)

**Where the drainage multiplier applies.** Only the dashboard's `/situation` call passes the district to `calculate_score`. The 3-hourly assessment (`/v1/alerts/assess`), `/score`, `/v1/districts/{district}/risk` and the risk history score the same rainfall without it, so for the three Accra districts the dashboard and the alert queue can show different scores for the same rain (section 9, M4).

**What the input rainfall is.** The 3-hourly assessment scores three signals separately for each district: (a) the next-24-hour Open-Meteo forecast; (b) the 3-day antecedent total, from GPM IMERG via the API (since 30 Oct 2026; before that CHIRPS, which was 18–30 days out of date, so every assessment fell back to Open-Meteo's past-72-hour model rainfall); and (c) the river/dam pathway score. The review threshold S ≥ 30 corresponds to 10 mm of rain in 24 hours, a common wet-season amount.

**One curve for two accumulation periods.** `POST /v1/alerts/assess` scores signal (b), a three-day total, with the same curve as signal (a), which was designed for 24-hour rain. Rain that is unremarkable over three days therefore reaches high tiers: 30 mm in three days scores 70 (CRITICAL, whose instruction reads "Prepare for flooding. Follow evacuation orders."). ### 2.4 Multi-source evidence fusion

The decision card fuses evidence in two stages (`src/models/multi_source_confidence.py`):

1. **Within a causal pathway:** a weighted mean of the readings, with a confidence derived from coverage (the share of expected sources present) and agreement (spread capped at 45 points). Coverage below 60% marks the result "degraded".
2. **Across pathways:** a noisy-OR gate (Pearl 1988), P(flood) = 1 − Π(1 − pᵢ), so one active pathway is not averaged down by another that is inactive.

**Pathways and source weights:**

| Pathway | Source | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pluvial | Rainfall (Open-Meteo forecast / observed) | 1.0 |
| Fluvial | River gauge level | 2.0 |
| Fluvial | Dam level, direct | 2.0 |
| Fluvial | Dam level, upstream proxy (e.g. Bagre for the north) | 1.2 |
| Direct observation | Sentinel-1 SAR, water detected | 2.2 |
| Direct observation | Sentinel-1 SAR, no water detected | 0.3 |
| Direct observation | Verified citizen reports (≥3) | 1.8 |
| Direct observation | Verified citizen reports (1–2) | 0.9 |

These weights are expert-set priors, not learned or skill-weighted, although the decision card's method text calls them "skill-and-coverage-weighted". The noisy-OR treats each 0–100 pathway score as a probability, which the rule score is not (S = 30 does not mean a 30% chance of flooding), so the fused value is an index, not a probability. A river at NORMAL level still contributes 15 points whenever it is available. A second, separate scheme ("forecast fusion", `src/models/forecast_fusion.py`) averages antecedent-rain, GloFAS and Flood Hub risks with equal weights for `/situation` and the dashboard; the two schemes can disagree and neither has been validated (section 9, M15).

**The fused tier does not drive the recommendation.** The decision card's recommended action and priority are looked up from the rainfall tier of `/situation` only. A river or dam flood with no local rain therefore yields "monitor" even when the fused tier is HIGH (section 9, H10).

**Sentinel-1 flood detection** (`src/hydrology/sentinel_processor.py`).

- **As deployed:** change detection between the median of images 60–30 days before the date and the median of the last 12 days (VH drop > 3 dB and VV drop > 1.5 dB; "water detected" above 0.1 km²) over `point.buffer(0.05)`. Earth Engine buffers in metres, so the analysis area is a 5 cm circle (0.0078 m²) with no pixel in it: water has never been detected, and every "no water detected" result so far is void. The per-district radius setting (0.05°, 0.08° for Kumasi and Tamale) is reported but never used.
- **Corrected in #74 (deployed 4 Nov):** a ±0.05° box around the centroid (±0.08° for Kumasi and Tamale; about 122–310 km²); before and after images from the same orbit direction; 30 m focal-median smoothing; sea and permanent water masked (JRC occurrence ≥ 50%); a flooded pixel must lose > 3 dB in VH and > 1.5 dB in VV *and* be below −20 dB in VH afterwards; detection needs ≥ 1 km²; analysed at 30 m.
- **Validation of the correction (one event, qualitative):** Mepe (North Tongu), 25 Oct 2023, during the Akosombo spill: 5.3 km² of new water, detected. The same window in 2024, without a spill: 0.4 km², not detected. All nine districts on 31 Oct 2026 (dry): 0.01–0.26 km². Radar sees little water between buildings, so urban flooding is largely invisible to it.
- **Confidence:** 0.55–0.90, rising with the distance of the mean backscatter change from the thresholds (a heuristic, not a probability).

**Dam spillage index** (`src/models/dam_spillage.py`): a fixed weighted formula,

> index = 0.5 × rainfall score + 0.3 × reservoir score + 0.2 × inflow score

where rainfall is normalised over 0–200 mm, reservoir level over 0–100% and inflow over 0–500 m³/s. It is a formula, not a fitted model. It is exposed only through `/api/dam/spillage`, which, with no inputs supplied, evaluates fixed defaults (180 mm, 95%, 450 m³/s for Akosombo), so it returns the same result on every call; it is not used in the risk score or the alerts.

**River and dam pathway score** (`src/hydrology/fluvial_pathway.py`, `altimetry_thresholds.py`). Each DAHITI water body is classified against percentiles of its own history and mapped to a score: below the 75th percentile NORMAL = 15; 75th–90th WARNING = 45; 90th–95th DANGER = 75; above the 95th FLOOD = 95. Because WARNING (45) exceeds the review threshold (30), any river or lake above its 75th percentile, a normal wet-season level, queues a MODERATE alert (section 9, M3). The percentiles come from each target's own record, with no link to flood dates and no seasonal adjustment.

### 2.5 Alerting, explanation and infrastructure

- **Human-in-the-loop alerting:** risk assessments create *pending* alerts, which an operator approves, dismisses or retracts on the dashboard.
- **Alert channels:** WhatsApp (Twilio) and Telegram (Bot API) are configured in production; SMS (Twilio) and email (SMTP) are implemented but disabled. Recipients are a configured list (empty in production) plus citizens who opted in by sending "ALERTS ON" (none so far; "ALERTS ON" without a place subscribes to all districts, at a fixed minimum tier of MODERATE). The WhatsApp sender is Twilio's shared sandbox number (`TWILIO_WHATSAPP_FROM` unset), which reaches only phones that have sent the sandbox join code, and WhatsApp accepts free-form business messages only within 24 hours of the user's last message unless an approved template is used; no template exists. **As deployed, WhatsApp cannot reach the public** (section 9, H3).
- **Message content:** English only: district, tier, score, rainfall and one generic sentence per tier. The response guidance and affected-community names stored with each assessment reach only the CAP file, and a retraction is sent through the same template, so it reads like a new flood alert (M11).
- **Approval:** approving, cancelling or dismissing a real alert needs the API key and, when configured, a second alert-approval key; approval is an atomic compare-and-set, so an alert cannot be sent twice. Approving an exercise alert never reaches the sending code. The reviewer is identified only by a typed name (M17), and an approval has no age check, so an assessment queued weeks earlier would be sent as current (M2).
- **Delivery record:** a send counts as delivered if any one provider succeeds. If every configured provider fails to start, the provider factory silently substitutes the mock provider, which always reports success, so an approved alert would be recorded as sent although nothing was sent (M21).
- **Alert format:** each alert is also served as an OASIS Common Alerting Protocol (CAP 1.2) document. Its sender is on a domain the project does not control, it has no `expires` element, the area is a list of community names without a polygon or geocode, the event is "Flood Warning" for every tier and certainty defaults to "Likely" (M10).
- **Exercise mode:** produces practice alerts that are never sent.
- **AI copilot:** answers questions about districts by calling the platform's own tools. Model: Google Gemini (`gemini-flash-lite-latest`).
- **Hosting:** FastAPI service on Google Cloud Run (europe-west1; exactly one instance, 1 vCPU, 2 GiB, 4 uvicorn workers; CPU allocated only during requests), with two SQLite databases (`alerts.db`, `community_reports.db`, WAL mode) continuously replicated to Google Cloud Storage by Litestream 0.5.17. API rate limits and alert cooldowns are designed to be shared through Upstash Redis; the Redis database no longer exists, so each worker currently keeps its own in-memory counters (section 9, M1). Merging to `main` does not deploy: a build and a `gcloud run deploy` by an authorised person are needed, and there is no deployment pipeline (M22).
- **Dashboard:** Streamlit, on Streamlit Community Cloud.

### 2.8 API and access control

The API has **54 operations** (OpenAPI specification generated from `src/api/main.py`). Access control:

- **API key required (17):** every operation that writes data or can send, approve, cancel or dismiss an alert; the alert queue and CAP export; the alert subscriber list; the copilot; and `POST /situation` and `POST /decision/card`.
- **Second key (alert-approval key):** additionally required to approve, cancel or dismiss a real (non-exercise) alert, when configured.
- **Signed webhooks (2):** `POST /webhooks/whatsapp` checks Twilio's request signature; `POST /webhooks/telegram` checks Telegram's secret token (constant-time comparison).
- **Public (35):** health checks, district registry, read-only risk, forecast, evidence, resources, decision, history, observations, predictions, data quality, verification and backtest results, the public scoring and explanation endpoints, and the dam spillage endpoints.

Request rate limits (slowapi) are set on only two operations: `/situation` (30 per minute) and `/v1/copilot/ask` (10 per minute). Public operations that run the full evidence pipeline (for example `GET /v1/districts/{district}/decision`) or recompute 36 years of Earth Engine data (`/v1/backtest`, `/v1/verification`) are not limited or cached (section 9, L1). The limits are keyed on the client address, but the container starts uvicorn with `--forwarded-allow-ips='*'`, under which uvicorn takes the address from the leftmost `X-Forwarded-For` entry, which the client writes; a caller can therefore change its apparent address on every request, while all dashboard users share the single address of Streamlit Cloud (L7). If `API_KEY` is unset in an environment other than production or development, the key check accepts every request; production refuses to start without it (L2). Until 4 November `GET /v1/community-reports` returned reporters' phone numbers without a key; since #70 was deployed it refuses such requests (checked 5 November; H4).

### 2.10 Operator dashboard and AI copilot

**Dashboard panels** (`hackathon/app/pages/dashboard.py`): header and data-source status; control panel (district, rainfall); executive summary; national map; evidence and confidence; impact assessment; operations (shelters, resources); decision centre (decision card); risk timeline (+6/+12/+18/+24 h); recorded risk history; AI copilot; alert review queue (approve, dismiss, retract, CAP download, exercise alerts); community-report triage; a full-screen public kiosk view (live forecast, auto-refreshing); and a demonstration mode that plays a scripted sequence of rainfall values through the real API.

**What the dashboard's figures mean.** Every panel except the kiosk is computed from the rainfall value in the control panel, so the dashboard is a scenario tool around the live forecast, not a display of measured conditions. The slider opened at a fixed 75 mm (score 100, EXTREME) whatever the weather; it now opens at the live 24-hour forecast and labels any other value "SCENARIO" (#72, merged). The impact panel's illustrative generator (`state_fallback.py`) was shown whenever the real estimate was zero, that is on every dry day; it is now shown only when the API returns no estimate (#71, merged). The review also found:

- **Public access with the approval key (C4).** The app has no viewer authentication and holds both the API key and the alert-approval key in its server-side secrets, so any visitor can open the review queue and approve or retract a real alert after typing any name, queue assessments, validate citizen reports and read reporters' names and messages. This defeated the purpose of the second key. Since 4 November (#75) the operator types the approval key each session; the app must also be made private, which on 5 November was not yet confirmed.
- **Constants shown as measurements (H7).** "Data Quality 92%" and "Active Sources 6" are fixed values shown in the header, summary and footer; per-source confidence bars are fixed (rainfall 85% whenever Open-Meteo answers, satellite 80% whenever the blind detector runs, river 78%, soil 72%); shelter status and capacity, rescue resources (3 boats, 5 ambulances, 10 pumps, 4 teams) and drive times are placeholders (disclosed in a caption); community and shelter markers on the map are placed at fixed offsets around the district centroid, not at their real locations. #75 replaces the two header tiles; the rest remains.
- **The score is shown as a percentage** ("Risk Score (%)", "Peak Risk 95%"), which reads as a probability; it is a 0–100 index (shown as /100 since #75).
- **The public kiosk (H5)** is an unattended screen that showed "MANDATORY EVACUATION ORDER" or "EVACUATION ORDER LIKELY" from the forecast-only rule score, without any human review. Evacuation is decided by NADMO and the district assemblies; since #75 the kiosk points to their instructions. When the forecast service fails, the kiosk's rainfall falls back to 0.0 mm, which displays as LOW risk: a silent all-clear (M23).
- **The risk timeline (H9)** scored the input rainfall plus the forecast; since the input is itself the 24-hour forecast, the +24 h point doubled it (dashboard, kiosk and copilot; fixed in #75, 4 Nov).
- **Copilot quick questions** asked "What roads will flood?" and "When will rain stop?", which no platform tool can answer (replaced in #75).
- **Deployed version:** the dashboard is built from `hackathon/app/pages/requirements.txt`, which pins Streamlit 1.54.0, so the platform's security upgrade to 1.66.1 never reached it (M19).

**Impact estimate** (`src/exposure/impact_estimator.py`). Population exposed = district population × (S/100) × 0.6; children, elderly, disabled and pregnant shares and school, hospital and market counts are project planning estimates; power substations = population/75,000. Populations for Tema, Kumasi, Tamale, Cape Coast, Ho and Sunyani are 2021 census figures; the three Accra districts are project estimates (they are not official units). It is a score-proportional planning heuristic, not an exposure model (no hazard map is overlaid on population).

**AI copilot** (`src/copilot/`). Google Gemini through the `google-genai` SDK, model alias `gemini-flash-lite-latest` (not a pinned version), with 8 tools that call the platform's own data (tracked districts, risk overview, decision card, evidence, forecast, resources, data-source health, data quality), at most 8 tool calls per question. The system instruction forbids stating any figure not returned by a tool in the same conversation. The answer is returned with the list of tool calls that produced it. The grounding rule is an instruction to the model, not a check in code, and the copilot's accuracy and grounding have not been evaluated against a question set (L4). On an error it returns the exception text to the user.

### 2.11 Data quality control

`GET /v1/data-quality` applies tests from the QARTOD real-time quality-control manuals (US IOOS): a gross-range test (rainfall 0–500 mm/day; soil moisture 0–0.9 m³/m³; water level −50–500 m) and a freshness test (altimetry 35 days + 2 days processing; SMAP 72 h), for the Open-Meteo forecast (Accra Central), the Tamale river level, the Lake Volta level and SMAP soil moisture. Flat-line and spike tests are implemented (`src/data_quality/quality_checks.py`) but not applied, and IMERG, GloFAS and Sentinel-1 are not checked (L3). `GET /v1/health/data-sources` reports whether each source is configured; it does not test that a configured key still works, so DAHITI shows "configured" while every call fails (H2). `/health/ready` always reports ready. Nothing monitors uptime or job failures: there is no uptime check and no log-based alert on Google Cloud (M8).

### 2.12 Verification and archives

- **Rule validation** (`src/models/historical_backtest.py`, `rare_event_verification.py`; `GET /v1/backtest`, `/v1/verification`; reported monthly by a scheduled job). The backtest counts a documented flood as "detected" when *any* day in the 60 days before it scores ≥ 30 (10 mm of rain), and reports the gap from that first day as "lead time"; in a rainy season this is almost always true, so its detection rate and lead times carry no information about skill. The SEDI verification scores the rule on 36.7 years of CHIRPS rainfall per district against the 7 days before each of the 8 documented floods (2007–2023): 1–5 events per district, too few for a stable estimate. Neither may be cited (M12); the rule's skill is measured in the research framework (M3, sections 5.2–5.4). `/explain/features` also states that an ML model "did not reliably outperform" the rule, which section 5.12 contradicts.
- **Prediction ledger and outcome verifier** (`src/verification/outcome_verifier.py`): 3–30 days after each prediction, four sources are searched for a flood in that district in the following 14 days (ReliefWeb, GDELT news, at least 3 verified citizen reports, a Sentinel-1 detection); any one confirms, and an automatic check overwrites any outcome a person has entered. Until 4 November the ReliefWeb and GDELT searches matched unrelated reports and the Sentinel-1 check could never confirm (C1, C3); the whole ledger was re-verified with the corrected searches on 4–5 November. Even when correct, the design gives a weak verification sample: a prediction every 3 hours with overlapping 14-day windows, and evidence sources that mostly cannot resolve a district (M13).

### 2.13 Governance, legal and ethical position

- **Mandate.** In Ghana, weather warnings are the responsibility of the Ghana Meteorological Agency (GMet), river-flood monitoring of the Ghana Hydrological Authority, and disaster response and evacuation of the National Disaster Management Organisation (NADMO) and the district assemblies. NFCC holds none of these mandates and is not a registered alerting authority (WMO Register of Alerting Authorities), so its CAP files cannot enter official alert feeds. WMO guidance calls for a single authoritative voice in warnings. The manuscript should present NFCC as decision support for these agencies, and the dashboard's self-description as a "National Emergency Operations Center" with a static "SYSTEM ACTIVE" should go.
- **Human oversight.** Every outgoing alert requires a person's approval and, in production, a second key. With shared keys and a typed reviewer name, the audit trail cannot establish who approved an alert; per-user accounts are needed before operational use (M17).
- **Personal data** (Data Protection Act, 2012, Act 843). The platform stores citizens' phone numbers, names, photos, locations and messages. There is no privacy notice at the point of collection, no retention or deletion policy, and phone numbers are written to Cloud Logging. These must be addressed, and registration with the Data Protection Commission considered, before public use (H4).
- **Emergency reports.** A citizen report containing "trapped" or "rescue" is classified CRITICAL but only produces a log line and an automatic reply; no operator is notified and the reply gives no emergency number (M18).
- **Research ethics.** The research uses only public, aggregate data and involves no human participants; the manuscript should state this and whether an ethics waiver was obtained (TRIPOD+AI item 17).

## 3. Research framework: study design and data

### 3.1 Study design

| Item | Main study (v2) | Sensitivity study (v1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spatial unit | 216 districts, FAO GAUL 2015 level 2, all 10 regions | 216 districts, same boundaries |
| Time unit | Monday–Sunday week | same |
| Period | 5 Jan 2015 – 28 Dec 2025 (573 weeks; 123,768 district-weeks) | 6 Mar 2000 – 9 Dec 2018 (979 weeks; 211,464 district-weeks) |
| Flood labels | Groundsource news-derived flood events, plus 8 documented events | Global Flood Database (MODIS), plus 8 documented events |
| Target | Flood onset (flooded this week, not flooded last week) | same |
| Scored district-weeks | 121,755 | 209,175 |
| Onsets | 898 in 167 districts (base rate 0.0074) | 203 in 68 districts (base rate 0.0010) |
| Test years | 2018–2025 | 2008–2018 |

Weeks that follow a flooded week (flood continuing or receding) are excluded from training and scoring, so every model is judged on predicting a **new** flood. The first four weeks of each district are excluded because their rainfall lags are incomplete.

### 3.2 Flood labels

- **Groundsource** (main study; Mayo et al. 2026; Earth Engine `projects/sat-io/open-datasets/groundsource_2026`): flood events extracted from news articles by a language model, each with a location polygon and start and end dates.
  - Ghana has 2,686 events.
  - Events with an area of 1,000 km² or less are assigned to the district containing their centroid (2,527 events).
  - 159 region- or country-level events are excluded.
  - A district-week is flooded if a placed event's dates overlap it.
- **Global Flood Database v1** (sensitivity study; Tellman et al. 2021; `GLOBAL_FLOOD_DB/MODIS_EVENTS/V1`): MODIS-mapped flood extents. A district-week is flooded if an event overlapping that week mapped at least 1 km² of water in the district, excluding permanent water.
- **Documented events:** 8 Ghana floods (2007–2023) in `src/hydrology/flood_polygons.py`, added to both label sets.
- **Sentinel-1 label audit** (section 5.8): an independent satellite check of the news labels. It is not used as a label.

## 4. Models and evaluation

### 4.1 Model catalogue

All models output P(flood onset) for a district-week. They are implemented in `research/flood_experiments/models.py` (scikit-learn 1.x, XGBoost 2.x).

| ID | Name | Algorithm | Inputs | Settings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | Climatology | Empirical onset rate by district and calendar month, shrunk toward the national monthly rate | District, month | Beta prior, strength 50 weeks |
| M2 | Persistence | 1 if the district flooded in weeks t−8 to t−2, else 0 | `flood_recent8` | – |
| M3 | NFCC rainfall rule | The live platform's score S (section 2.3) on the week's wettest day, ÷ 100 | `rain_week_max` | Not fitted |
| M4 | River-discharge percentile (GloFAS-style benchmark) | Percentile of the week's maximum discharge within the district's training weeks | `q_week_max` | Not fitted |
| M5 | Logistic regression, rainfall | L2-regularised logistic regression (Cox 1958) | SEASON + RAIN | C = 1.0; standardised inputs; missing values imputed with the training median |
| M6 | Random forest, rainfall | Random forest classifier (Breiman 2001) | SEASON + RAIN | 300 trees, min_samples_leaf = 30, max_features = √p |
| M7 | XGBoost, rainfall | Gradient-boosted trees (Chen & Guestrin 2016) | SEASON + RAIN | XGBoost settings below |

**XGBoost settings (M7–M12):**

- **Trees:** up to 600, learning rate 0.03, maximum depth 3.
- **Regularisation:** min_child_weight 10, L2 penalty λ = 5.
- **Sampling:** row and column subsampling 0.8 each.
- **Early stopping:** after 50 rounds without improvement in validation PR-AUC.
- **Monotone constraints:** risk may only increase with every rainfall, soil-moisture, runoff and discharge input.

**Data-fusion strategies (experiment E5):**

| ID | Strategy | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Early fusion | One model on all inputs (= M9) |
| F2 | Late fusion (mean) | One logistic regression per source group, probabilities averaged. Groups: rainfall (SEASON + RAIN + RAIN_LAGS), soil (SEASON + SOIL), river (SEASON + RIVER), static (GEOGRAPHIC + EXPOSURE) |
| F3 | Stacking | The F2 members combined by a logistic-regression meta-model fitted on the validation year |
| F4 | NFCC expert weights | The platform's hand-set fusion: (1.0 × rainfall score + 2.0 × river score) ÷ 3.0. The river score is (q_ratio − 1) ÷ 2, limited to 0–1 |

**Calibration methods (E7):** Platt scaling (Platt 1999) and isotonic regression (Zadrozny & Elkan 2002), fitted on the validation year.

**Explanation method (E9):** SHAP TreeExplainer (Lundberg & Lee 2017; Lundberg et al. 2020).

### 4.2 Validation design

- **Temporal forward-chaining (E1, E2, E3, E5–E7):** for each test year y, train on all years ≤ y−2, validate on y−1, test on y. Predictions are pooled over the test years.
- **Spatial hold-out (E4):** leave one region out, *and* separate it in time. Train on the other regions up to two years before the first test year, validate on them the following year, then test the held-out region over the test years. A purely spatial split would leak, because one flood event covers several regions in the same weeks.
- **Decision thresholds:** chosen to maximise F1 on the validation year, then applied unchanged to the test year. When the validation year has fewer than 5 onsets, the training years are used instead.
- **Uncertainty:** 90% intervals from a block bootstrap that resamples whole years (300 resamples), because weekly flood series are correlated within a season.

### 4.3 Evaluation metrics

| Metric | Definition | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ROC-AUC | Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve | Hanley & McNeil 1982 |
| PR-AUC | Average precision; equals the base rate for a no-skill model | Saito & Rehmsmeier 2015 |
| Brier score | Mean squared error of the probabilities | Brier 1950 |
| ECE | Expected calibration error, 10 equal-count bins | Naeini et al. 2015 |
| POD, FAR, MER, POFD | Hit rate; false-alarm ratio; missed-event rate (1 − POD); probability of false detection | Wilks 2011 |
| SEDI | Symmetric Extremal Dependence Index | Ferro & Stephenson 2011 |
| POD / FAR ±1 week | Event-based: an onset counts as detected if the district had an alert in weeks t−1 to t+1; an alert is false only if there was no onset within ±1 week | – |
| Within-district AUC | ROC-AUC within each district, averaged with weights by the district's onsets | – |
| Relative economic value | Value V(α) for a user with cost-loss ratio α, at the best threshold; 1 = perfect, 0 = no better than always or never acting | Richardson 2000; Murphy 1977 |

### 5.8 Label audit with Sentinel-1

**Design.** Every news-reported onset was checked, plus two matched no-flood controls:

- the same week in a randomly chosen other district;
- the same district and week of the year in another year.

**Method.** UN-SPIDER Recommended Practice change detection on Sentinel-1 GRD, VH polarisation, ascending passes, over ±1 week:

- **Reference:** the dry-season (January–February) median of the same year.
- **Water:** after/reference > 1.25 (in dB) and after < −20 dB.
- **Masks:** JRC permanent water (≥10 months a year), slope ≥ 5° (HydroSHEDS), and clusters smaller than 8 pixels, on a 100 m grid.
- **Confirmed:** at least 1 km² of new water in a district that was at least half imaged.

### 5.11 Satellite rainfall against station gauges

**Why.** Every rainfall input in the study (CHIRPS v2.0) and the live platform (GPM IMERG V07 for antecedent rain) is satellite-based. Neither TAHMO nor GMet data were available (section 6), but GMet's synoptic stations are exchanged internationally through the WMO and republished by NOAA in the Global Surface Summary of the Day (GSOD). This checks both products against those gauges.

**Data and method** (`research/flood_experiments/station_check.py`):

- **Gauges:** NOAA GSOD daily precipitation for the 22 Ghana stations active since 2015, 2015-01-01 to 2025-08-31.
- **Gauge quality control:** of 62,545 station-days, only complete 24-hour totals are used (flags D, F, G: 16,668 days). Flag I (38,028 days) means no precipitation report, not a measured zero; A, B, C, E and H are partial-day sums; 99.99 is missing.
- **Satellite:** the value of the 0.05° CHIRPS cell and the 0.1° IMERG cell containing each station (IMERG daily total = sum of the 48 half-hourly rates × 0.5 h; days with fewer than 48 half-hours dropped).
- **Day alignment:** a lag test gave correlations of 0.22 (same day) and 0.33 (gauge day matched to the previous satellite day) for IMERG, so the gauge's 24-hour total ends in the morning and gauge day *d* is compared with satellite day *d* − 1.
- **Weeks:** Monday-to-Sunday totals, only for weeks with all 7 gauge days.
- **Two gauge definitions:** *strict* (D/F/G only) and *I as dry* (also counting flag I as 0 mm). The stations send the 24-hour group mainly on rainy days (3% of January days have a complete flag, 45% of September days), so strict days are skewed wet and give few complete weeks (156); counting I as dry gives 2,355 weeks. On I days both products average about 1.5 mm, against about 4.6 mm on strict 0.0 mm days, so I days are mostly, but not all, dry.
- **Metrics:** Pearson r and Spearman ρ; volume ratio (satellite total ÷ gauge total); POD, FAR and CSI for rain ≥ 1 mm, for ≥ 20 mm, and for each series' own station-wise 95th percentile of wet days (a detection test that a volume bias cannot distort); for weeks, r, the correlation of anomalies from each station's week-of-year mean, and detection of each series' own wettest 10% of weeks.

**Interpretation.**

1. **Daily point agreement is low** for both products (r about 0.26–0.33), and heavy-rain days are poorly detected: of gauge days with ≥ 20 mm, CHIRPS also had ≥ 20 mm on 22% and IMERG on 33%, with false-alarm ratios of 0.69 and 0.63. Part of this is the mismatch between a rain gauge (a point) and a 5–11 km grid cell during convective storms, not satellite error alone.
2. **Weekly agreement is good** (r 0.75–0.76 strict, 0.66 with I as dry) and survives removal of the seasonal cycle (anomaly r 0.53–0.57). This supports the district-week design of the study.
3. **IMERG Final matches the gauges at least as well as CHIRPS** on every daily measure. The live platform, however, uses near-real-time IMERG (flagged `provisional` in Earth Engine from 1 October 2025), which has no gauge calibration and was not part of this check. The result supports IMERG as a product family, not the specific live input.
4. **Volume:** on strict days the satellite totals are close to the gauges (ratio 0.88 CHIRPS, 0.97 IMERG). With flag I counted as dry the satellites exceed the gauges by about 40%, which is the upper bound of the bias, since some I days had rain.
5. **For the live rule:** a daily threshold applied to satellite or model rainfall (the rule's 10 mm review threshold) will miss most heavy local rain and raise many false alarms at the scale of a town; applied to a three-day total, it fires on a large share of all days (section 2.3).

**Caveats.** CHIRPS blends GTS station reports, which include these stations, so its agreement is partly built in; IMERG Final is adjusted to monthly GPCC gauge totals. Neither comparison is fully independent; IMERG is the closer to it. All IMERG days compared are Final (`permanent`) images. GSOD ends on 24 Aug 2025 for most stations and has no data for Ada after 2022. A gauge measures a point and a satellite cell an area of 30–120 km², so part of the daily disagreement is representativeness, not satellite error.

### 9.2 Strengths, and why the tests missed the critical defects

**Strengths.** No alert can be sent without a person approving it; exercise alerts never reach the sending code; approvals are atomic (no double sends) and the second approval key is enforced by the API; CAP 1.2 is produced; webhooks are signature-checked; secrets other than the Redis URL are in Secret Manager; most data adapters report "unavailable" with a reason instead of a substitute value; continuous integration blocks merges below 80% test coverage; database replicas restore automatically on start-up; and the research framework is reproducible end to end from open data, with time-separated validation and no leakage found (features, climatologies and thresholds use only data available before each test year; the deployed model uses only inputs known when the week starts).

**Why the tests did not catch C1–C3 and H1.** Each defective module has tests, but they mock the external service with the developers' own understanding of it, so they confirm the code against the same misunderstanding. The remedy is contract tests built from recorded real responses, and a scheduled smoke test against the live services: for example, Sentinel-1 must detect the 2023 Mepe flood, the GloFAS ratio must vary over a year, and a ReliefWeb query for a district must not return reports from other countries.

## 11. Limitations to state in the manuscript

1. **Label completeness and bias.**
   - News-derived labels follow media coverage: urban districts and recent years are over-represented (Groundsource has 124 Ghana events in 2015 and 338 in 2022).
   - Satellite radar confirms rural labels but cannot verify urban ones (section 5.8).
   - MODIS-based labels (v1) miss most urban and flash floods.
   - Event dates come from publication dates, so an onset can be misplaced by a week; the ±1-week scores (section 4.3) partly absorb this.
2. **Place versus timing.** Much of M11's advantage comes from knowing which districts flood (built-up share is the leading SHAP feature). For timing alone (within-district AUC), the NFCC rainfall rule (M3) is as good as the best model (section 5.12).
3. **Lead time.** Most results use observed rain for the target week (a perfect-forecast hindcast). With real forecasts (E3c), feeding the forecast directly lowers skill; the best real-time model uses no rain forecast; and the NFCC rule applied to real forecasts has almost no skill. E3c has only two test years.
4. **Flood types.** Pluvial, fluvial and coastal floods are pooled in one label. The models have no tide, storm-surge or sea-level input, so coastal flooding (Keta, Ada, Cape Coast) is predicted, if at all, only through rainfall and place.
5. **Resolution.** The unit is the district-week. Sub-daily flash-flood timing and street-level extent are not modelled.
6. **Boundaries.** FAO GAUL 2015 (216 districts). Ghana has had 261 districts since 2018.
7. **Missing drivers.** Dam releases, station rainfall and river gauges are unavailable (section 6).
8. **Rare events.** 898 onsets (v2) and 203 (v1); eight test years in the main study. Intervals are wide, and several model differences cannot be resolved (section 5.12).
9. **Expert-set parts of the live platform.** The live platform's fusion weights, drainage factors, river/dam percentile bands, dam formula, tier thresholds and lead-time table are expert-set, not validated.
10. **Rainfall at the scale of a town.** Satellite rainfall agrees with station gauges week by week but poorly day by day, and detects under a third of heavy-rain gauge days (section 5.11). The near-real-time IMERG used live was not part of that check.
11. **Point sampling in the platform.** Forecasts, GloFAS and soil moisture are read at one point per district (the centroid), which for GloFAS can be a minor channel rather than the main river.
12. **Satellite flood detection** in the platform is validated on one event only (#74, live since 4 November), and radar sees little urban flooding.
13. **No operational record.** The platform has not yet been used by operators or the public (section 8); no claim about warning delivery, response or impact can be made.
14. **Verification of live predictions.** The live prediction log has no valid outcomes (C3); live skill can be claimed only from the research hindcasts and, later, from shadow-mode verification.
